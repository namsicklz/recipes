Ebi Katsu Sando (Japanese Shrimp Cutlet Sandwich)

Source: feedthepudge.com. Crispy panko-coated shrimp cutlets in soft shokupan (Japanese milk bread) with tangy tartar sauce and cabbage.

Note: the full ingredient/step breakdown was generated as a PDF directly from the source page rather than pasted into chat — pull the full recipe from feedthepudge.com's "Ebi Katsu Sando" page if remaking without the PDF.

What it includes

  • Shrimp cutlets (panko-breaded, fried)
  • Tartar sauce (often made with Japanese Kewpie mayo)
  • Shredded cabbage
  • Shokupan (Japanese milk bread)

What it is

"Ebi" means shrimp, "katsu" means panko-breaded deep-fried cutlet, "sando" is just Japanese for sandwich. Instead of whole shrimp, the shrimp are chopped, mixed with a binding ingredient, and formed into patties for a tender interior / crispy exterior contrast. Patties are dredged in flour, egg, and panko, then deep-fried until golden, and assembled between shokupan slices with tartar sauce and cabbage. A variation of the broader katsu-sando family (usually pork or chicken), adapted here with shrimp — some versions swap in tonkatsu sauce or spicy mayo alongside the tartar.
