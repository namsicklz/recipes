Cowboy Soup

Source: Chelsea's Messy Apron (Chelsea Lords). Makes 6 servings. Prep 40 min, cook 40 min, total 1 hr 20 min. Combines the best of chili and vegetable soup — ground beef, tender potatoes, plenty of veggies, and a deeply flavorful broth.

Equipment

  • Large pot

🧂 Ingredients

  • 1 tbsp olive oil, plus more as needed
  • 1 lb ground beef chuck (80/20)
  • 1½ cups finely diced yellow onion (1 large onion)
  • 1½ cups finely diced carrots (4 carrots)
  • 1 large red bell pepper, finely diced
  • Salt and pepper
  • 1 tbsp minced garlic (3 cloves)
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1 tsp each: beef bouillon powder, garlic powder, onion powder, ground cumin, dried oregano
  • 2 tsp each: chili powder, paprika
  • ¼ cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 (14 oz) can crushed tomatoes (good-quality/fire-roasted recommended — see notes)
  • 2 dried bay leaves
  • 1 (32 oz) carton beef broth (4 cups — see notes)
  • 2 cups diced gold potatoes, unpeeled is fine
  • 1 cup frozen corn
  • 1 (15 oz) can black-eyed peas, drained and rinsed
  • 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
  • 1 tbsp red wine vinegar
  • ¼ cup finely chopped parsley, optional, for topping
  • Freshly shredded sharp cheddar, optional, for serving
  • Crusty buttered bread, optional, for serving

👩‍🍳 Instructions

  1. Heat oil in a very large, heavy-bottomed pot over medium-high heat. Add beef to the center, season to taste (~½ tsp each salt and pepper). Sear, then crumble and cook until deeply browned. Remove with a slotted spoon, loosely cover with foil, set aside.
  2. You want ~2 tbsp fat left in the pot — drain excess or top up with olive oil to reach that. Add onion, carrots, and red pepper. Sauté, stirring occasionally, until softened and lightly browned, 7–10 minutes.
  3. Add garlic, tomato paste, beef bouillon, garlic powder, onion powder, cumin, oregano, chili powder, and paprika. Cook, stirring constantly, until fragrant and the tomato paste darkens slightly, 2–3 minutes.
  4. Sprinkle flour over everything, stir well, cook 1 minute stirring constantly. Add crushed tomatoes (careful, they may sputter) and cook 1 more minute. Gradually pour in beef broth while stirring constantly, scraping up browned bits from the bottom.
  5. Add potatoes and bay leaves, stir well. Increase heat to high, bring to a boil. Reduce to medium-low, cover, simmer 15–20 minutes until potatoes are fork-tender — stir every 4–5 minutes, scraping the bottom so nothing catches, then re-cover promptly.
  6. Once potatoes are tender and broth has thickened, return beef to the pot. Add black-eyed peas, corn, and Worcestershire sauce; stir until warmed through. Stir in red wine vinegar, taste, adjust seasoning (typically another ¼–½ tsp salt). Simmer longer over low heat for more flavor/thicker texture if desired.
  7. Discard bay leaves. Ladle into bowls, top with parsley and cheddar if using. Serve with crusty buttered bread.

📝 Notes

  • Tomatoes matter here — good-quality crushed tomatoes, fire-roasted especially good (Cento Marzano, Carmelina Marzano Italian, or Muir Glen). With cheaper tomatoes, may need 1–2 tsp sugar at the end to balance acidity.
  • Beef broth quality matters too — Swanson recommended.
  • Storage: cooled leftovers keep airtight in the fridge up to 4 days; flavor improves as it sits. Reheat gently on the stovetop or microwave, splash of broth to thin if needed.

📊 Nutrition (per serving)

425 cal, 22g protein, 43g carbs, 19g fat (6g saturated), 8g fiber, 12g sugar, 1041mg sodium.
