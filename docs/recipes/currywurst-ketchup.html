Currywurst Ketchup

Two versions: the authentic Berliner street-stand classic, and an elevated "Michelin-level" take. Both are savory, tomato-forward, and mild — not a sweet Americanized ketchup or a hot chili sauce.

Version A — Authentic Berliner Style

Yield: ~2½ cups

Ingredients

  • 2 tbsp neutral oil (sunflower or canola)
  • 1 small onion, very finely minced
  • 2 cloves garlic, finely grated or minced
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1 cup crushed tomatoes (passata preferred)
  • ½ cup water
  • 2 tbsp apple cider vinegar (or mild white wine vinegar)
  • 2 tbsp sugar (traditional; use less if desired)
  • 1 tsp sweet paprika
  • 1 tsp mild curry powder (German-style if possible)
  • ½ tsp smoked paprika (optional but common)
  • ¼ tsp ground cumin
  • ¼ tsp ground coriander
  • ¼ tsp white pepper
  • ¼ tsp black pepper
  • ¼ tsp mustard powder
  • Salt to taste (~¾ tsp)

Instructions

  1. Heat oil over medium-low. Add onion with a pinch of salt, cook gently until very soft and translucent (8–10 min), no browning.
  2. Stir in garlic and tomato paste, cook 1–2 minutes until the paste darkens slightly.
  3. Add crushed tomatoes, water, vinegar, sugar, and all spices except curry powder. Bring to a gentle simmer.
  4. Simmer uncovered 20–25 minutes, stirring occasionally, until thick but pourable.
  5. Blend until completely smooth.
  6. Return to low heat, stir in curry powder, simmer 2 more minutes. Adjust salt, sugar, or vinegar.

Serve: grill or pan-sear bratwurst/bockwurst, slice into rounds, spoon warm ketchup over, dust generously with extra curry powder, serve with fries or a crusty roll.

Authenticity notes: no ketchup bottles (it's a tomato sauce, not Heinz-based), no heat-heavy chili, curry powder added at the end never early.

Version B — Michelin-Level Street Food

Yield: ~2½ cups

Elevations: caramelized tomato paste for depth, onion and apple for natural sweetness, curry layered in stages, finished with butter for gloss, strained for velvety texture.

Ingredients

  • 2 tbsp neutral oil (sunflower preferred)
  • 1 small yellow onion, very finely minced
  • 1 small apple (Granny Smith or Braeburn), peeled & micro-diced
  • 2 cloves garlic, finely grated
  • 3 tbsp tomato paste (double-concentrated if possible)
  • 1½ cups high-quality passata (or crushed San Marzano tomatoes, blended)
  • ½ cup water or light chicken stock
  • 2 tbsp apple cider vinegar
  • 1½ tbsp sugar
  • 1 tsp honey (optional)
  • 1½ tsp sweet paprika
  • 1 tsp mild German curry powder
  • ½ tsp smoked paprika
  • ¼ tsp ground cumin
  • ¼ tsp ground coriander
  • ¼ tsp white pepper
  • ⅛ tsp ground ginger
  • ⅛ tsp ground cinnamon
  • ½ tsp mustard powder
  • Fine salt, to taste (~¾ tsp)
  • 1 tbsp unsalted butter, to finish
  • Extra curry powder, for dusting

Instructions

  1. Heat oil over medium-low. Add onion, apple, and a pinch of salt. Cook gently 10–12 minutes until completely soft and jammy, no browning.
  2. Add garlic and tomato paste, cook 2–3 minutes, stirring constantly, until darkened and smelling roasted.
  3. Add passata, stock/water, vinegar, sugar, honey, and all spices except curry powder. Simmer uncovered 25–30 minutes until thick and cohesive.
  4. Blend until completely smooth, then pass through a fine sieve.
  5. Return to low heat, stir in curry powder and butter. Simmer 2 minutes, taste, adjust.

Serve like a pro: grilled bratwurst/bockwurst rested after grilling, sliced into bite-size rounds, sauce spooned warm (never poured), fine dusting of curry powder, optional micro-chives, served with double-fried pommes or a crisp roll.

Variations: Spicy Ruhr style (¼ tsp Aleppo pepper), Smoky Cart version (½ tsp smoked curry powder), Vegan Michelin (veg stock, olive oil instead of butter).
